About

A statistics & data science student on the neuroscience track, currently deciding whether the most interesting variable in the room lives in a spreadsheet or a scan.

I'm a rising sophomore at Carnegie Mellon University studying Statistics and Data Science with a Neuroscience Track and a minor in Neural Computation. My work so far has moved between clinical time-series (ICU mortality prediction from MIMIC-IV data), sports neuroscience (biomechanics and brain injury modeling), and NLP (annotating visual hallucinations in multimodal AI systems) — different data, same instinct: find the model that actually explains what's happening to a person.

I'm especially drawn to brain-computer interfaces and computational modeling of Parkinsonian and other neurodegenerative disease states — the idea that a well-built model of a struggling nervous system could translate directly into a device that gives someone back a bit of the control or communication their disease has taken away. It's where my statistics background and my neuroscience curiosity actually meet.

Outside the lab, I'm the treasurer for CMU Science Olympiad, a member of the Data Science Club, and a dancer with the Kpop Dance Club.
